01One beam, seven colours

Everything you need.
Split into seven.

White light goes into a prism and leaves as a spectrum. Prism does the same with a browser: everything it does, spread out where you can see it.

  1. Ads, trackers, cookie banners and the ads inside YouTube videos, gone before they load. Seventeen filter lists and uBlock Origin's page scripts, built in, with no extension running.

  2. Open sites your network blocks. The helper ships inside Prism: use it in Private, for every site, or only the sites you choose. If it can't get through, the page falls back to your normal connection.

  3. A thin band at the foot of the sidebar. Hover for a glance at your tabs then; click for your day in chapters, and bring a whole moment back. Kept only on your Mac, never in Private.

  4. Tabs you aren't using really go to sleep, even the ones full of ad frames that keep other browsers awake. Pinned and favourite tabs stay ready until your Mac actually needs the memory.

  5. One shortcut and there's nothing left but the page. Touch the window's left edge and the sidebar slides back.

  6. Peek at a link in a floating glass window without losing your place. Keep it as a tab, or let it go.

  7. One field for everything. Type an address, search, or jump to any open tab, setting or action.

04A note on AI

No agents. No chatbots. Just a browser.

Here's the twist: most of Prism's code was written by AI, steered by one person with strong opinions. Building it that way made the AI browser playbook easy to see through. A browser should get out of the way, not get between you and the web.

  • An agent that clicks around the web for you
  • A chatbot glued to every page
  • “Magic” buttons that read all your tabs
  • Sponsored tiles on the new tab
  • An account just to get started

A few small helpers, like naming Spectrum's chapters, stay quiet until you use them. They run with Apple Intelligence on your Mac, or with your own Claude key.
